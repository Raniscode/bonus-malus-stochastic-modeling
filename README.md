# History-Dependent Bonus-Malus Systems

## Overview

This project explores the application of stochastic modeling and simulation to automobile insurance, focusing on bonus-malus systems that adjust insurance premiums according to drivers' claim histories.

Conventional Markov chain models describe how policyholders transition between insurance classes over time. However, drivers in the same class may have different claim histories and potentially different risk profiles.

The project investigates whether incorporating historical claims information can improve the representation and prediction of policyholders' future behavior.

## Objectives

- Study the mathematical foundations of Markov chains and bonus-malus systems.
- Implement a conventional Markov-based insurance model in Python.
- Develop an extended model that accounts for historical claims information.
- Simulate policyholder behavior and compare the models' results.
- Analyze transition probabilities, long-run distributions, expected premiums, and predictive performance.
- Document the findings and develop research papers based on the theoretical and experimental results.

## Methodology

The project will combine mathematical analysis, Python implementation, and stochastic simulation. Initial experiments will use simulated data, with assumptions documented explicitly. Both models will be evaluated under comparable conditions to investigate the effects of incorporating additional historical information.

## Research Direction

The central research question is:

**Does incorporating historical claims information improve the modeling and prediction of future risk in automobile bonus-malus systems?**

The project will begin with a review of existing actuarial research and progressively develop research questions, experiments, and analyses. Any claims of improvement will be supported by experimental evidence.

## Project Status

Currently in the initial research and planning phase. Mathematical foundations, literature review, model design, implementation, and experimental evaluation will be developed progressively alongside the Stochastic Modeling and Simulation course.

## Research Outputs

The intended outputs include a reproducible Python project, documented experiments, a mathematical analysis, and one or more research papers presenting the methodology, findings, and limitations.

## Disclaimer

This project is for educational and research purposes. Initial results based on simulated data do not constitute validated insurance pricing recommendations.
